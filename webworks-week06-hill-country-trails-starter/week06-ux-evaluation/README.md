# Week 6 — UX Evaluation Brief

## Hill Country Trail Guide

**Primary User:** Maya Torres  
**Primary Task:** Choose a beginner-appropriate Saturday hike that can be completed in about three hours or less.

---

## 1. Task Walkthrough

Briefly describe what Maya would try to do first, what she would look for, and where she might hesitate.

Maya would first skim the website, looking for a call to action in the hero section and the information on the trail cards to pick an appropriate trail. She would likely be looking for estimated completion time, difficulty, and trail details on the trail cards. In the information section she would look for recommended supplies and park information (fees, access, parking, dog policy), and further information on the trail experiences explained simply. She might hesitate at the beginning of this process due to the lack of instruction in the hero section, and lack of certain information being made clear about the trails in both the cards and info section.

---

## 2. Five UX Findings

Document **exactly five meaningful findings**.

### Finding 1

**Observation:**  
The hero section has a lack of clear instruction on what to do next.

**Evidence:**  
While the hero text tells the user to find/choose a trail, there are no buttons or elements standing out telling the user where to go to complete this task.

**User Impact:**  
This could confuse Maya and make her unsure of how she is supposed to follow the call to action.

**Principle:**  
Clarity and navigation.

**Priority:** High

**Recommendation:**  
Add some sort of instruction telling the user where to go next to follow the call to action, or add a link/button that directly takes them there.

### Finding 2

**Observation:**  
Trail cards do not use all provided categories.

**Evidence:**  
Trail cards only use the difficulty categories provided, not the 'dog friendly' or 'under 5 miles' categories.

**User Impact:**  
These labels can act as an easy way for the user to see what trail details are most appropriate for them. Maya would not be able to find an appropriate trail through skimming due to the cards lacking these labels.

**Principle:**  
Clarity and Error Prevention

**Priority:** High

**Recommendation:**  
Add 'dog friendly' and 'under 5 miles' labels to the trail cards, similar to the existing difficulty labels.

### Finding 3

**Observation:**  
Navigation links do not lead anywhere.

**Evidence:**  
The links in the navbar do not bring the user to any new site locations or pages when clicked.

**User Impact:**  
Since the navbar is often looked to for easily finding information associated with the link labels, having one that does not work can be confusing and deprive Maya of the ability to easily and quickly find information.

**Principle:**  
Navigation and Interaction Clarity

**Priority:** Medium

**Recommendation:**
Add pages or spots on the page for the navbar items to link to.

### Finding 4

**Observation:**  
Trail information lacks estimated completion time.

**Evidence:**  
Average or estimated time taken to complete a trail is not provided in the cards or information section.

**User Impact:**  
Maya specifically wants a trail that can be completed in around 3 or less hours. She has no way to see this information and choose a trail accordingly, and may lead her to choose a trail that takes more time than she wanted.

**Principle:**
Clarity and Error Prevention.

**Priority:** High

**Recommendation:**  
Add estimated time to complete a trail to the trail cards.

### Finding 5

**Observation:**  
Park information is missing or has odd placement on the website.

**Evidence:**  
Parking information is provided only in the Juniper Creek Loop's section of information, and all three trail information sections reference park information without this information being made known elsewhere on the site.

**User Impact:**  
The lack of rules, fees, and open hours provided on the site makes it so Maya can't know important information required for visiting the park, and the placement of the parking information may make it confusing to find.

**Principle:**  
Clarity, Hierarchy, and Error Prevention.

**Priority:** High

**Recommendation:**  
Include a section for information about the park so that important details can be easily found by users.

---

## 3. Top Three Priorities

Identify the three findings that should move forward into Week 7.

1. Add instruction to the hero section, such as a sentence or button, so that the next steps for the user are more easily understood. This will help Maya understand what is next with picking a trail.
2. Add more information to the trail cards, such as estimated completion time and whether or not they are dog friendly. These are important details that will help Maya decide on an appropriate trail.
3. Move and add important park information to the site so that details such as information on parking, fees, and rules are easily found. This will allow Maya to know important things about the park ahead of time.

For each, briefly explain why it matters to Maya's primary task.
